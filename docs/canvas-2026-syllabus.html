Course purpose

This applied, research-facing course is designed for senior undergraduates, graduate students, and researchers who want to build defensible machine-learning systems for Earth data. We work with time series, tables, geospatial imagery, gridded fields, and point observations from seismology, oceanography, the cryosphere, planetary science, geology, geodesy, and related fields.

The goal is not merely to obtain an accurate model. You should be able to explain your data choices, prevent leakage, select meaningful baselines, quantify uncertainty, reproduce your work, and defend every scientific claim.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  • prepare diverse geoscience observations as documented, AI-ready data sets;
  • build reproducible environments and version work with Git and GitHub;
  • choose between supervised and unsupervised methods based on the scientific question and data;
  • construct and train classic ML and deep-learning models;
  • design leakage-aware temporal, spatial, and grouped validation;
  • evaluate models with appropriate baselines, uncertainty, and calibration;
  • track data, models, and experiments so another researcher can rerun the work;
  • evaluate AI-agent output using task specifications, ground truth, and failure analysis;
  • disclose AI assistance and retain responsibility for every submitted choice; and
  • communicate results to technical and nontechnical audiences and assess downstream consequences.

Materials and computing

The open course book contains the readings, tutorials, notebooks, and homework specifications. Students work in VS Code or JupyterLab, manage environments with pixi, use Git/GitHub for version control, and use PyTorch and scikit-learn for modeling. Bring a charged laptop to every class meeting. If you need a device, see UW’s Student Technology Loan Program.

Each student creates a course repository named MLGEO2026_UWNETID as directed in the course book. Never commit credentials, tokens, private data, or data you do not have permission to share.

AI assistants and academic responsibility

AI assistants are part of the subject of this course and may be used within the rules stated for each assignment. You remain responsible for all submitted work. You must be able to explain and defend every methodological choice, verify factual claims and citations against primary sources, test generated code, protect private data and credentials, and disclose assistance using the course’s required format. Fluent output is not evidence of scientific correctness.

Submission, late work, and collaboration

Each student may use one extension of up to two calendar days on one homework assignment. Request it at least 48 hours before the deadline whenever possible. Time-limited quizzes, in-class activities, peer-feedback work, presentations, and the final project deadline are not covered by this automatic extension. Approved accommodations and serious circumstances are handled separately; contact the instructor as early as possible.

Submit work through the destination named on the Canvas assignment. A GitHub link is not submitted until it is entered in Canvas when the assignment asks for one. Discussion and peer help are encouraged when permitted, but submitted work must identify collaborators and assistance. Do not share solutions, fabricate results or citations, or represent generated work as independently verified work.
