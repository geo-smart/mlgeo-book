## What this course is about

Machine Learning in the Geosciences is an applied, research-facing course for
senior undergraduates, graduate students, and researchers who want to build
defensible machine-learning systems for Earth data. We work with time series,
tables, geospatial imagery, gridded fields, and point observations from across
the geosciences.

The course develops four connected capabilities:

1. turning raw observations into AI-ready data;
2. training and evaluating classic machine-learning models;
3. building and diagnosing deep-learning models;
4. working critically and transparently with agentic AI assistants.

The goal is not merely to obtain an accurate model. You should be able to
explain your data choices, prevent leakage, select meaningful baselines,
quantify uncertainty, reproduce your work, and defend every scientific claim.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- prepare diverse geoscience observations as documented, AI-ready datasets;
- build reproducible environments and version work with Git and GitHub;
- choose between supervised and unsupervised methods based on the scientific
  question and data;
- construct and train classic ML and deep-learning models;
- design leakage-aware temporal, spatial, and grouped validation;
- evaluate models with appropriate baselines, uncertainty, and calibration;
- track data, models, and experiments so another researcher can rerun the work;
- evaluate AI-agent output using task specifications, ground truth, and failure
  analysis;
- disclose AI assistance and retain responsibility for every submitted choice;
- communicate results to technical and nontechnical audiences and assess
  downstream consequences.

## AI assistants and academic responsibility

AI assistants are part of the subject of this course and may be used within the
rules stated for each assignment. You remain responsible for all submitted
work. You must be able to explain and defend every methodological choice,
verify factual claims and citations against primary sources, test generated
code, protect private data and credentials, and disclose assistance using the
course's required format.

The full policy and disclosure examples are in the course book's AI-use policy.
An assistant's fluent output is not evidence that the output is scientifically
correct.

## Submission, late work, and extensions

Each student may use one extension of up to two calendar days on one homework
assignment. Request it at least 48 hours before the deadline whenever possible.
Time-limited quizzes, in-class activities, peer-feedback work, presentations,
