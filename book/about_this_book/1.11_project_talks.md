# Giving a project talk

This page is for the project proposal talks (Fri Oct 30) and the final presentations (Dec 11 and finals week). A first talk rarely follows all of it; the feedback you receive will refer to these points so you can improve the next one.

## One question, one message

Before making slides, write two sentences:

1. **The question**: which Earth system, which quantity, which gap. "Can daily GNSS positions at Cascadia stations separate snow loading from tectonic motion?" is a question; "ML for GNSS" is a topic.
2. **The message**: what the audience should remember when you leave the room. For a proposal, it is why the question matters and why your data and approach can answer it.

Every slide should serve one of the two sentences. If it serves neither, cut it.

## Structure of a 6-minute proposal talk

| Slide | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title, team, and the question in one sentence | 0:30 |
| 2 | The system and why the question matters: a map or a figure of the phenomenon | 1:00 |
| 3 | The two closest published studies and how your project differs from each | 1:00 |
| 4 | Your data corpus: where it comes from, what one sample is, a figure of the data | 1:00 |
| 5 | AI readiness: your scores on the 2.13 rubric that matter most, and the out-of-domain test you plan | 1:00 |
| 6 | The plan: baseline, method, how you will measure success, and the main risk | 1:00 |
| | Questions | 2:00 |

## Slides

- **A title that states the point.** "El Niño is the first EOF of tropical Pacific temperature" tells the audience what to see; "SST results" does not.
- **One figure per slide, as large as the slide allows**, with axis labels and units readable from the back of the room (at least 18 pt). Say where the data come from on the slide.
- **Few words.** If you read a sentence aloud from a slide, the audience reads ahead of you. Put the details in your speaker notes.
- **Numbers with units and their source.** "P395 moves 6.4 mm/yr west (NGL MIDAS, IGS20)" rather than "slowly".
- **A map whenever the data have locations**, and the scale of the system (km, years, magnitudes).
- **The same template for all speakers**: fonts, colors, and figure style should not change when the speaker changes.

## Delivery

- **Rehearse aloud at least twice, with a timer.** Talks run 20 to 30% longer than planned the first time.
- **Speak to the audience, not the screen.** Point at the part of the figure you are discussing, then turn back.
- **Explain each figure before its meaning**: what the axes are, what one point or line is, then what it shows.
- **Slow down on numbers and on the question.** Pause after the sentence you want people to remember.
- **Finish on time.** Stopping at 6 minutes is part of the grade; cut a slide rather than speed up.

## Two or more speakers

- **Split by part, not by slide.** For example, one speaker presents the question, the system and the literature; the other the data, the readiness scores and the plan. Each speaker talks for at least two minutes.
- **Hand off with a sentence that connects the parts**: "Those two studies used one network; Ana will show why our data can test the method elsewhere." Avoid "next slide please" and "I'll pass it to my partner".
- **Rehearse the transitions**, not only your own part. The hand-off is where shared talks lose time.
- **One person advances the slides**, or each speaker uses the clicker for their own part; decide in advance.
- **Prepare for questions together.** Decide who answers questions on the science, the data, and the methods, and let the other add one sentence at most.
- **Stand where you can both see the screen and the audience**; the speaker who is not talking stays still and looks at the slides or the speaker.

## Answering questions

- Repeat or rephrase the question so everyone hears it, and so you answer the question that was asked.
- Answer in one or two sentences, then stop. "We do not know yet; we will test it by ..." is a good answer.
- Write down the questions afterwards: they show what your audience did not understand.

## How talks are given feedback

Feedback uses the presentation criteria of the [final project](1.10_MLGEO_FinalProject.md): content, structure, clarity and delivery, visual aids, engagement, critical thinking, and time. For the proposal talks the feedback is formative: it is there to prepare the final presentations.
