# Project proposal rubric

The project proposal (due Fri Oct 30) is a written proposal and a 6-minute talk. This page is the rubric the instructors use to grade it, and the one your team should use to check its own work before submitting. It grades content, not style: write the proposal in any format (a Google Doc, Markdown in your repository, a Jupyter Book page) and design the slides as you like, as long as every criterion below can be found and checked.

The rubric follows the same idea as the [AI-ready data rubric](../Chapter2-DataManipulation/2.13_MLready_data.ipynb): each criterion is scored **0, 1 or 2**, and each score is earned by something a reader can verify in your proposal, your repository or your talk.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **2** | Everything the criterion asks for is present, specific to your project, and checkable. |
| **1** | Present but incomplete, generic, or not yet checkable. |
| **0** | Missing, or a claim that cannot be verified. |

Each criterion has a weight in points; your points for a criterion are its weight times the score divided by 2. The total is 10 points.

## The criteria

### P1. Scientific question (2 points)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Names the Earth system and one question about it (what to measure, predict or discover), says why it matters for the science or for a decision, and states the problem in general terms: inputs, target (or none, if unsupervised), and what result would count as success. | A topic rather than a question ("machine learning for landslides"), or the inputs, target or measure of success are missing. | No question, or the question is about a method rather than the Earth. |

### P2. Literature review and novelty (1.25 points)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| At least five published studies, found with the help of an AI assistant and **each verified**: the DOI resolves and the title, authors and year match. The proposal says how the search was done (which assistant, which queries) and states plainly whether the question has already been answered. | Fewer than five studies, the search process is not described, or the novelty statement is vague. | Any citation that cannot be verified (a wrong DOI, a paper that does not exist, a mismatched author list), or no literature. **One unverifiable citation scores 0 on this criterion.** |

### P3. The two nearest neighbors (1.25 points)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Two studies identified as the closest to your project, each summarized in one or two sentences (data, method, evaluation, main result). A table compares your project with each on data, region or period, method, evaluation and question, and one sentence of the form "Unlike [A] and [B], we …" names a difference that is specific and true. | The two studies are not the closest available, the summaries miss data or evaluation, or the difference is generic ("we use deep learning"). | No nearest neighbors, or the difference claimed is not supported by the table. |

### P4. Data corpus and AI readiness (2 points)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Provider, version and license of each dataset; what one sample is; how many independent units there are (stations, events, years, basins). The corpus is scored on the 11 criteria of the AI-ready data rubric (A1 to A4, B1 to B7) with one line of evidence each. The two lowest scores are named with a plan to raise them, and the planned out-of-domain test is stated with its axis (statistical, spatiotemporal or physical) and why it fits the question. | Data described but without license, version or the count of independent units; rubric scores without evidence; or no stated out-of-domain test. | Data not identified, or not accessible to the team. |

### P5. Reproducible start (1 point)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| A link to the team's GitHub repository, which contains the course environment (`pixi.toml` and `pixi.lock`), a smoke test that runs with `pixi run`, an `AGENTS.md`, and instructions for downloading the raw data. A reader can clone it and run the smoke test. | Repository exists but one of these pieces is missing or does not run. | No repository, or it is private to the reader. |

### P6. Plan and roles (1 point)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| A baseline (the simplest method that could answer the question), the machine-learning approach to try, the metric and why it matches the question, the main risk with a fallback, a timeline to the December presentation, and who does what (CRediT roles, every member named). | A method without a baseline, a metric not tied to the question, or roles missing. | No plan. |

### P7. Talk (1 point)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Within 6 minutes; every team member speaks; follows the structure of [Giving a scientific talk](1.11_project_talks.md) (question, system, nearest neighbors, data and readiness, plan); hand-offs between speakers connect the parts. | Over time by more than a minute, one member does not speak, or a part of the structure is missing. | No talk. |

The talk also receives written, formative feedback (see below). Only the points above count toward the grade.

### P8. AI-use disclosure and reflection (0.5 points)

| 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Which tools were used and for what, what was verified and how, and specific answers to the three reflection questions: what you learned from this exercise, what you learned from interacting with agents or chatbots, and what you would change in this assignment so it meets its learning goals. | Disclosure or reflection present but generic ("we used ChatGPT to help"). | Missing. |

## Feedback on the talk

This is the first talk of the quarter. Instructors and classmates give written feedback on these points, with a comment for each; it does not change the grade, and the same points return, graded, in the December presentation ([final project](1.10_MLGEO_FinalProject.md), Presentation).

| Point | What the listener checks |
|---|---|
| Opening | The question about the Earth is clear within the first minute. |
| Story | The gap is stated before the plan; the talk ends on one sentence, not "Questions?". |
| Slides | Each title states a claim; figures are readable from the back (labels 18 pt or larger), with units, scale bars and maps where relevant. |
| Delivery | Speakers face the room, pace allows the audience to read each figure, and numbers come with units. |
| Two speakers | Hand-offs connect the parts in a sentence; both speakers can answer questions on the whole project. |
| Timing | Within 6 minutes; time is spent on the question and the data, not the title slide. |
| Questions | Answers are direct; "we don't know yet" is acceptable when true. |

## Check your proposal with an assistant

Before submitting, you may give this page and your proposal to an AI assistant and ask it to score each criterion. Copy this request, then check every score it gives against your own reading; an assistant can miss a missing piece or invent one that is there.

```text
Score the attached project proposal on the rubric below. For each criterion P1 to P8:
give 0, 1 or 2; quote the sentence or table from the proposal that justifies the score;
if the evidence is not in the proposal, write "not found" and score 0 rather than guess.
For P2, list every citation and say whether you could confirm its DOI, title, authors and year;
do not mark a citation as verified unless you checked it.
End with the two changes that would raise the total the most.
```

Note what the assistant got wrong in your AI-use disclosure (P8).
