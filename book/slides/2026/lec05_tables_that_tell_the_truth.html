A rock survey with planted truth

🧪 Sample & laboratory 🪨 Solid Earth ⛰️ Geology & geomorphology

10,000 rock samples: 7 major-element oxides, density, magnetic susceptibility, lithology

985 andesites, against 5,525 granites: a 5.6 : 1 class imbalance

Synthetic whole-rock geochemistry (mlgeo_synth): the generator plants the correlations and the pathologies, so every cleaning decision is graded against known truth.

Imbalance is the norm in geoscience labels. Remember 985 when a classifier reports 90% accuracy in Chapter 3.

Introduce the table: each data sample is one rock, with oxides in weight percent, bulk density, magnetic susceptibility, and a lithology label (granite, basalt, andesite) that a classifier will later predict. The badges describe what the table imitates, laboratory analyses of hand samples; the science tag says it is synthetic, and no map applies because the samples have no locations. Why synthetic: 2.4’s point is that repairs get graded. The notebook knocks out 2% of densities and 3% of susceptibilities at random and plants 15 density zeros, then checks whether cleaning recovers the truth. The imbalance seeds exercise question 3: a model that always says “granite” scores 55% accuracy (5,525 of 10,000). Physical sanity checks come first in the notebook: oxides between 0 and 100 wt%, crustal densities roughly 2.5 to 3.3 g/cm³. Read describe() against physics, always.

A zero is not one thing

Column Zeros in the table Verdict
MgO 1,434 real: evolved granites have fractionated their magnesium away
K2O 824 real: same logic, other rock types
density 15 impossible: no rock has zero density, so this is an instrument’s failure code

The check: is zero inside the physically possible range of this variable?

“Replace all zeros with missing” would delete 1,432 silica-rich granites. That rewrites the geology; it does not clean it.

The tempting shortcut is a global zeros-to-missing sweep; walk through why it fails, column by column. The MgO zeros sit almost entirely in granites (1,432 of 1,434), and those granites are silica-rich: median SiO2 of 74.2 wt% against 69.2 for the full table. That is textbook petrology: evolved melts run out of Mg, so the zeros carry the geology. The 15 density zeros are the opposite. They are physically impossible, so they are sentinel codes, a database’s way of writing “no measurement” inside the data values. Real archives use 0, −999, −9999; the fingerprint is a heap at an impossible or suspiciously round value. Fix: replace only the impossible ones with an explicit missing marker. Numbers rerun on Oct 7 from mlgeo_synth.geochem_table(n=10000, seed=42) and the notebook’s planting step.

Censored values look right and are wrong

Synthetic (mlgeo_synth): a displacement series moving at 12 mm/yr, reported through a 5 mm floor.

12% of samples sit exactly at the 5 mm floor, yet a missing-data check reports zero problems. Censored data passes every missingness test.

Define censoring aloud: the instrument reports a number, but the number is the reporting floor, not the measurement. Geochemical analyses write “<0.01” as 0.01; stream gauges floor out at low flow. Be straight about the example: a GNSS position has no such floor, so this is a GNSS-shaped series with a floor imposed to make the bias measurable; the real cases are laboratory detection limits (trace elements, nutrients, contaminants) and gauges at low flow. Left panel: the reported series (red) rides the dashed floor whenever the true series (gray) dips below it. Right panel: the fingerprint, a heap of identical values exactly at the limit, the same signature as the sentinel zeros one slide ago, but at a value that is plausible instead of impossible. That plausibility is what makes censoring dangerous: nothing looks broken. The generator returns the uncensored truth, so the next slide can measure the damage exactly.

What the floor does to the science

Series used for the fit Trend (mm/yr) Year-1 mean (mm)
True, uncensored 12.44 4.80
Reported, floor values kept 11.59 7.24
Reported, floor values dropped 11.82 9.29

Both shortcuts bias in the same, predictable direction. Dropping makes the early mean worse and the trend slightly less wrong: neither recovers the truth.

Model the censoring, or report the limit in the data card. Never substitute or drop and move on.

The bias is not noise; it has a sign you can reason out. Censoring only ever pulls low values up, so first-year statistics inflate and the trend deflates. Ask the room to predict the third row before revealing it. Dropping removes the low tail entirely, so the year-1 mean rises further (9.29 against 7.24, truth 4.80); the trend improves a little (11.82 against 11.59) because the dropped samples were the ones dragging the early fit up, but it is still 0.6 mm/yr low, and which statistic suffers depends on what you compute. That is the lesson: neither shortcut is safe in general. Honest options: survival or tobit-type methods that treat “below L” as the datum it is, or at minimum reporting the limit alongside the data so downstream users can. Related trap: a mid-record instrument swap changes the floor mid-series, so a step in a record’s variance is a metadata question before it is a discovery. All six numbers rerun on Oct 7: mlgeo_synth.gnss_series(n_years=4, velocity_mm_yr=12.0, seed=7) through degrade_series(detection_limit_mm=5.0, seed=7), least-squares line on 1,461 daily samples (1,286 after dropping).

The gap can be the data

  • The stream gauge drowns in the flood it was built to measure
  • The GNSS antenna goes silent under the storm’s snow load
  • The field crew skips the location when the road washes out

Missing completely at random (MCAR): safe to drop. Missing because of the value (MNAR): dropping deletes your extremes.

Ask what process made the gap. If the answer involves the measurement, the missingness belongs in your data card as data.

Rubin’s vocabulary, on geoscience instruments. The planted gaps in 2.4 are MCAR by construction; that is what licenses dropna in the notebook, and the text says so. Real instruments rarely oblige: each bullet is a case where the gap is caused by the value that would have been measured, so dropping rows systematically deletes floods, storms, and washouts, the events the analysis probably exists to study. Connect backward: the MgO zeros were the same idea inverted, “suspicious” values concentrated in one geology, so removing them rewrites the field. The operational habit: for every gap pattern, write one sentence on its mechanism; if the sentence mentions the measured quantity, the missingness policy is a scientific decision to defend, not a default.

The dtype trap

  • Cleaning density: replace the impossible zeros with a missing marker. Correct.
  • pandas silently changed the column’s storage type: float64 → object (numbers stored as generic objects)
  • Every later “numeric columns only” step (correlations, summaries) now drops density without a warning

A column can be numeric in content and non-numeric in dtype. Check dtypes after every cleaning step, not just the first.

The notebook’s most instructive bug, planted deliberately. The cleaning that protected density (zeros to missing) changed its storage type; from then on, any select-numeric-columns operation, the correlation matrices and describe(), silently excludes the column the cleaning was defending. No error, no warning; the analysis quietly loses a variable. The fix is one cast back to numeric, but the lesson is the habit: dtype checks are part of every step. This is also the modern-pandas moment: assign results (df = df.replace(…)) rather than mutating in place, because chained in-place edits on slices raise errors in pandas 3. Downstream payoff of a clean table: the correlation matrices show the planted geochemistry (SiO2 anticorrelates with FeO, MgO, CaO), plus the closure effect: oxides sum to about 100 wt%, which builds anticorrelation into the table regardless of petrology. That subtlety is exercise question 1.

Four pathologies, four fingerprints

Pathology Fingerprint Geoscience example Response
Sentinel value heap at an impossible value density = 0 g/cm³ replace only the impossible ones
Censored value heap at a plausible round value laboratory detection limit model it, or report the limit
Informative gap gaps track events gauge drowned in the flood treat the gap as data
Dtype drift numeric content, non-numeric dtype density after cleaning check dtypes every step

Same discipline throughout: diagnose the mechanism first, clean second, write the decision down.
