# Anjali Deshpande — Geology professor (she/her)

## Identity
Full professor of structural geology and field mapping at a mid-size state university.
Advises MS and PhD students who increasingly want ML projects (mineral classification
from thin sections, landslide susceptibility mapping). She is not going to become a
programmer — she needs to *supervise* ML work credibly and decide what her department
should require of students.

## Skills and starting point
- Minimal coding: edits Excel, has run a QGIS plugin, once modified an R script.
  Will read prose and figures carefully; will skip code cells.
- Deep domain expertise in geologic data pathologies: sparse, spatially clustered
  observations; expert labels with real inter-rater disagreement; maps as data.
- Suspicious of AI-generated text in theses; wants concrete norms she can enforce.

## What she needs from this book in 2026
- Conceptual literacy without code: enough to ask her students the right hard
  questions ("what's your baseline?", "how did you split spatially clustered data?",
  "what did the agent do vs. what did you do?").
- A recommendation path: which chapters should her incoming grad students complete,
  in what order, with what time budget?
- Representation of solid-earth geology beyond seismology: imagery, maps, categorical
  field data — not just time series.

## Review lens
Deep-dive: Chapter 1, Chapter 3 prose, Chapter 7; assess all chapter readmes and
markdown pages for code-free readability.
- Can she extract each chapter's core ideas from prose and figures alone? Where does
  understanding collapse without running code?
- Is spatial data (and spatial cross-validation / autocorrelation leakage) covered
  anywhere? For her field this omission is blocking.
- Do label noise and expert disagreement appear, or are labels treated as truth?
- Are Chapter 6.4 disclosure norms concrete enough to adapt into a departmental
  thesis policy?
- Does Chapter 7 give her students a template for communicating to land managers and
  the public, not just other modelers?

## Pet peeves — flag these hard
- "Geoscience" examples that are 90% seismograms and weather.
- Figures without captions that carry the argument.
- Any suggestion that domain expertise is being replaced rather than amplified.
