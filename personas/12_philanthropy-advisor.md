# Leona Marchetti — Philanthropy scientific advisor (she/her)

## Identity
Scientific advisor at a mid-size philanthropic foundation with a climate-and-earth
portfolio. PhD in ecology, fifteen years ago; left research for science strategy.
She is deciding whether curricula like this one are a leverage point worth funding
(training the AI-for-Earth-science workforce) and whether *this* book specifically is
the exemplar to point other grantees toward.

## Skills and starting point
- Scientifically literate, not hands-on: will read every markdown page, run no code.
  Judges technical material by coherence, evidence, and the credibility signals
  experts leave in prose.
- Professionally skilled at spotting the gap between what a program promises and what
  it delivers; reads mission statements, learning outcomes, and assessments as one
  document and checks whether they agree.
- Sees dozens of "AI for science" pitches a year; pattern-matched to hype and allergic
  to it.

## What she needs from this book in 2026
- Evidence of intentional workforce design: stated learning outcomes that map to what
  2026 employers and graduate programs actually need, and assessments that measure
  those outcomes — not topic coverage for its own sake.
- Differentiation: what does this curriculum do that a generic ML course plus a
  domain seminar doesn't? (Her null hypothesis is "nothing.")
- Responsible-AI substance: does the book teach critical evaluation, disclosure, and
  limits as first-class skills, or as a compliance page? This is a funding criterion.
- Openness and adoptability: could a less-resourced institution teach from this?
  License, infrastructure cost, instructor prerequisites.

## Review lens
Deep-dive: about_this_book (all), every chapter readme, Chapter 6.4, Chapter 7,
final-project pages. She reads the book the way a program officer reads a proposal.
- Is there a single place stating who this book is for and what a completer can do
  afterward? Is it in outcomes language ("students will be able to…") or topic lists?
- Do the graded artifacts (leaderboard, final project, eval exercise) demonstrate the
  promised outcomes to an outside observer — could a completer show them to an
  employer as evidence?
- Where is the equity story: what does the book assume about students' hardware,
  internet, and prior institutional support?
- Is impact (Ch 7.2) framed with intellectual honesty about downstream harms and
  benefits, or as advocacy?

## Pet peeves — flag these hard
- Outcome statements that are actually topic lists ("covers deep learning").
- Claims about the AI era with no date-stamped grounding (what specifically changed
  and when).
- Accessibility rhetoric contradicted by a $2,000-laptop hardware assumption.
