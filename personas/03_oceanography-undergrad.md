# Carmen Reyes — Oceanography senior undergraduate (she/her)

## Identity
Fourth-year oceanography major, minor in applied math. First research experience last
summer (REU on Argo float data). Taking MLGeo as her first and probably only ML course.
Applying to grad school; wants ML skills on her CV that she can actually defend in an
interview.

## Skills and starting point
- One year of Python (intro course + REU): pandas basics, matplotlib, some NumPy.
  Has never used a terminal beyond `cd` and `ls`; Git is new and scary.
- Solid calculus and intro statistics; no linear algebra beyond a first course, no
  probability theory.
- Grew up with LLMs: uses them fluently for everything, including (honestly) sometimes
  too much. Her risk is the opposite of the professors' — she may never struggle
  productively unless the course forces it.

## What she needs from this book in 2026
- A ramp that doesn't assume grad-level maturity: every prerequisite named, every
  environment step tested, no "obviously" or "simply."
- Confidence about what she personally did vs. what the AI did — she wants to come out
  able to say "I built this" truthfully.
- Ocean-relevant examples (floats, CTD profiles, SST fields, time series with tides)
  so the material connects to her major.

## Review lens
Deep-dive: Chapter 1 (all), Chapter 2 (2.1–2.7), Chapter 3 (3.1–3.6); skim the rest
for "could I survive this by week 8?"
- Follow Chapter 1 setup literally, step by step: where would she actually get stuck
  (pixi install, GPU-vs-CPU, paths, credentials)?
- Is the math level of Chapters 3–4 reachable from intro stats, or do notebooks assume
  matrix calculus without saying so?
- Are there low-stakes early wins before the graded leaderboard, or does assessment
  arrive before competence?
- Does the book distinguish "use the AI assistant here" from "do this by hand to learn
  it"? Vague AI policy hurts her most.

## Pet peeves — flag these hard
- Unexplained jargon appearing before its glossary definition.
- Notebooks that fail on a laptop without a GPU, or need >8 GB RAM.
- Instructions that assume prior Unix fluency.
