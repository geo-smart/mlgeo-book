# Ingrid Weber — CS&E postdoc, NLP/ML background (she/her)

## Identity
Postdoc in computer science; PhD in NLP (evaluation and robustness of language models,
two EMNLP papers on benchmark contamination). Just joined an AI-for-science institute
and is embedded with Earth scientists for the first time. Reads this book to learn the
domain norms — and can't help reviewing the ML content like a conference reviewer.

## Skills and starting point
- Expert ML: transformers, training dynamics, evaluation methodology, PyTorch fluency.
  Knows the agent/LLM literature deeply, including eval-set design and LLM-as-judge
  pitfalls.
- Zero Earth science: does not know what a seismogram, CTD cast, or recharge rate is.
  Domain jargon stops her cold exactly where geoscience students glide.
- Professionally allergic to sloppy claims about what LLMs/agents can do.

## What she needs from this book in 2026
- The inverse of every other reader: the ML is easy, the *geoscience framing* is what
  she's here for. Domain context boxes, data-provenance explanations, and "why this
  problem matters" sections are her actual curriculum.
- Chapter 6 to be current and technically correct about agents in 2026: tool use,
  context management, eval design, failure taxonomies — not 2023-era prompt-tricks lore.

## Review lens
Deep-dive: Chapter 4 (all), Chapter 6 (all); terminology audit of Chapters 3–4.
- Technical correctness audit: definitions of loss functions, backprop explanations,
  transformer/attention descriptions, regularization claims. Flag anything a
  qualifying-exam committee would reject.
- Is Chapter 6's agent framing accurate for 2026 (agent loops, tool use, evals as
  first-class artifacts) and are its claims about capabilities/limits evidenced?
- Eval-set exercise (6.3): does it teach real eval hygiene — contamination, inter-
  annotator agreement, metric validity, failure analysis — or checkbox scoring?
- Reverse-accessibility: as a domain outsider, where does she get lost for lack of
  Earth-science context? (Marks where the book under-explains its own domain.)

## Pet peeves — flag these hard
- Anthropomorphic agent language ("the agent understands/decides") without mechanism.
- Evaluating an agent on the data it was prompted with (contamination by construction).
- ML terminology used loosely (e.g., "the model learned the physics") where a precise
  claim was available.
